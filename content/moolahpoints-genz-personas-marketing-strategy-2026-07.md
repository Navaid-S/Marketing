# Moolah Points — Gen Z / Young Adult Customer Personas & Capture Strategy
**Prepared:** July 21, 2026
**Scope:** Consumer-facing personas for shoppers enrolling in a Moolah Points loyalty program at a liquor store, smoke/vape shop, or convenience store — not the store-owner buyer persona (covered separately in `moolahpoints-market-research-2026-07.md`).

---

## 0. Read this first — a legal/compliance correction to the brief

The request was to target "Gen Z and younger crowd who are over 18." For two of Moolah Points' three core verticals, **18 is not the legal or platform-compliant threshold — 21 is.**

| Category | Federal minimum age | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Alcohol | **21** — National Minimum Drinking Age Act (1984), enforced in all 50 states | Standard federal law |
| Tobacco & vapor products | **21** — federal "Tobacco 21" law, signed December 2019 | FDA retail tobacco enforcement |
| General convenience-store merchandise (snacks, drinks, most non-restricted goods) | No age restriction | N/A |

This isn't just a legal line — it's an active platform-enforcement line. As of 2026, **Meta requires all US alcohol ads to be targeted 21+ (25+ for any featured influencer), with age-gating enforced on every linked landing page, lead form, and Messenger flow.** **TikTok goes further and doesn't allow retail/e-commerce ads promoting alcohol sale at all**, and requires a visible "21+" badge in the first frame of any alcohol-adjacent video content it does permit. [Sources: [HawtAds, Meta Alcohol Advertising Policy 2026](https://hawtads.com/blog/meta-alcohol-advertising-policy-2026-age-targeting-21-in-the-united-states-explained-3), [AuditSocials, TikTok Alcohol & Gambling Ads 2026](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/tiktok-alcohol-gambling-restricted-industry-advertising-policy-2026)]

**What this means practically:** the personas below split into two groups. The liquor and smoke/vape personas target **legal-age young adults, roughly 21-27** — the legal-purchasing end of Gen Z (Gen Z spans birth years ~1997-2012, so in 2026 the legal-age slice is ~21-29; we focus tighter on 21-27 since that's where the behavioral data below is richest). The convenience-store persona is the one place an 18-20 audience is genuinely fair game, because it covers general merchandise, not age-restricted product.

This document proceeds on that corrected basis. Every recommendation below assumes strict 21+ gating on any alcohol- or tobacco/vape-adjacent content, consistent with the PACT Act/SHAFT/TCPA guidance already published in Blog #2.

## 1. Personas

### Persona A — "The Discovery Drinker" (Liquor vertical, ages 21-27)

**Who they are:** Just aged into legal drinking within the last few years, or in their mid-20s and increasingly deliberate about it. Drinks less often than prior generations did at the same age, but treats each drinking occasion as more intentional — small-format cocktails, a "daycap" instead of a big night out, trying something new rather than defaulting to the usual.

**What the data says about them:**
- Not drinking less so much as **drinking differently** — favoring low-ABV spritzers, dessert-inspired cocktails, and smaller pours over volume. [Ask Attest, Gen Z Alcohol Trends 2026]
- Awareness and interest in sub-3% ABV beer/cider is up year over year (49% awareness, 47% interest, both climbing); low-ABV cocktails now have 63% awareness and 51% interest. [Ask Attest]
- **54.5%** "often" or "sometimes" choose non-alcoholic or low-alcohol alternatives; another 22% are curious but haven't tried yet. Only a minority are fully outside this trend. [Ask Attest]
- **43% say they're more likely to try a new drink product if it's marketed around the sober-curious lifestyle** — moderation-friendly framing is a positive purchase driver, not a niche concern. [Ask Attest]
- A third believe alcohol is "generally unhealthy," and many are fluent in the concept of "hangxiety" — this generation grew up with public-health messaging prior generations didn't. [Ask Attest]

**What they want from a loyalty program:** Not a bigger discount for buying more — that's the wrong lever entirely for a generation moderating volume. They respond to **discovery** (new products, limited releases, a reason to try something), **gamified engagement** (challenges, instant digital rewards, leaderboards — 46% of Gen Z engage more with gamified programs than flat-discount ones), and **early access** over pure price-off. [Advantage Club, Antavo Gen Z Loyalty 2026]

**Where they are:** Instagram has the highest reach (89% use it, highest time allocation), TikTok drives the most product discovery (77% use TikTok specifically to find products), and Discord/BeReal are where the more private, high-trust engagement happens. [Sprout Social, Hootsuite Gen Z Statistics 2026]

### Persona B — "The Switch-Curious Regular" (Smoke/vape vertical, ages 21-27)

**Who they are:** A regular at a smoke or vape shop, but the relationship with the product category is more complicated than "loyal nicotine consumer." Young adults 18-29 have the highest vaping prevalence of any age group, but this same cohort is also the most quit-motivated.

**What the data says about them:**
- Adults 18-29 report the highest vaping prevalence of any age bracket (18%, vs. 6% who smoke cigarettes) — vaping, not cigarettes, is this cohort's dominant nicotine product. [SingleCare, Vaping Statistics 2026]
- **67% of young adult nicotine users aged 18-24 say they plan to quit.** [Truth Initiative, 2026] This is the single most important fact for how Moolah/retailer content should be framed in this vertical — a majority of the customer base is actively trying to reduce or exit the category, not looking to be marketed deeper into it.
- A notable emerging counter-trend: some doctors report **cigarettes regaining social appeal with people in their 20s** even as vaping remains the bigger category overall — a reminder that behavior here is shifting fast and shouldn't be assumed static. [UVA Health / WWBT, July 2026]
- Product variety (flavor, device type, nicotine strength options) and non-nicotine adjacent categories (glass, CBD, accessories) are real parts of smoke-shop basket composition, not just repeat nicotine purchases.

**What this means for loyalty design:** This is the one persona where the "more purchases, more rewards" default loyalty logic is in real tension with both the customer's own stated goals and brand-safety/regulatory optics (FTC and state attorneys general actively scrutinize marketing that could be read as encouraging youth or first-time nicotine use). The safer, more defensible, and arguably more loyalty-building design is to **reward variety, accessories, and category-adjacent purchases** (device upgrades, glass, CBD/non-nicotine product lines) rather than pure consumption-volume incentives, and to keep messaging squarely on the store relationship (selection, expertise, service) rather than product-consumption prompts. This isn't just risk-avoidance — it's also more consistent with the compliance-authority positioning already built into Blog #2 and the content strategy's Pillar 1.

**Where they are:** Same platform mix as Persona A (Instagram reach, TikTok discovery), but with a strong SHAFT-filtering constraint on any SMS content mentioning tobacco/vape products directly (see the compliance guide) — campaigns need to route through a landing page rather than naming products in the text itself.

### Persona C — "The C-Store Discovery Regular" (Convenience vertical, ages 18-24 — the one persona where 18+ genuinely applies)

**Who they are:** The convenience store isn't a fallback stop for this group — it's a destination in its own right. This is the persona with the most upside and the least regulatory friction, since it covers general merchandise, not age-restricted product.

**What the data says about them:**
- **3 in 4 Gen Z adults visit a convenience store at least once a week**, and **92% say they enjoy browsing** — this cohort treats the c-store as a browsing destination, not just a quick transaction. [Convenience Store News / NACS, 2026]
- **9 in 10 say they browse on purpose**, usually looking for a small indulgence or something new to try — discovery-seeking behavior, not routine restocking. [NACS]
- They shop by **mood, need, and occasion** rather than traditional category (snacks/meals/beverages) — "energy" is the fastest-climbing demand signal, growing at nearly 2x the prior year's rate. [Tastewise via NACS]
- **1.5x more visually driven** than older shoppers when trying a new snack — packaging and shelf presentation matter disproportionately to this group. [NACS, Sweets & Snacks 2026]
- Functional sodas, protein-forward snacks, and "better-for-you" positioning are all climbing, alongside continued strength in indulgent/nostalgic treats — this cohort wants comfort, energy, and novelty simultaneously, not one at the expense of the others. [NACS]

**What they want from a loyalty program:** Because there's no age-restriction friction here, this is the persona to be most aggressive and playful with — gamified discovery mechanics (a "try something new" bonus-points trigger fits naturally with the 92%-enjoy-browsing data point), visually-driven social content, and the lowest-friction possible enrollment (phone number only, instant first reward) to capture a shopper who's already treating the store as a destination.

**Where they are:** Heaviest TikTok/Instagram Reels overlap of all three personas, plus the strongest fit for UGC-style content (snack-haul videos, "what's new at my corner store" browsing content) since this is organic, native behavior for this age group already, not something that needs to be manufactured.

## 2. The "perfect way to capture" this audience — strategy

### 2a. Structural principle: let the compliance line do the segmentation for you

Don't build one "Gen Z campaign" spanning all three verticals. Build the **c-store persona as the wide-open acquisition engine** (18+, no restrictions, highest natural enthusiasm per the browsing/discovery data above), and treat liquor/smoke-shop content as **21+-gated retention and upsell** for the same customer once they age in or are already of legal age. A shopper who enrolls in Moolah Points at 19 buying energy drinks and snacks is a warm, already-enrolled contact by the time they turn 21 — that's a genuinely valuable lifecycle advantage a competitor starting cold at 21 doesn't have, and it's fully compliant because the initial enrollment touchpoint never involved an age-restricted product.

### 2b. Loyalty mechanics: gamification over discounts, discovery over volume

The data is consistent across all three personas: flat discounts and "buy more, save more" mechanics are the weakest lever for this cohort. What works instead:
- **Gamified challenges and instant digital rewards** — QR scavenger hunts, streak bonuses, leaderboards. 46% of Gen Z respond more to gamified programs than flat-discount ones. [Advantage Club]
- **Early access over price-off** — first access to a new SKU, a limited flavor drop, or a new product category outperforms a percentage discount for this cohort's stated preferences. [Antavo]
- **"Try something new" triggers** — directly maps to the 92%-enjoy-browsing / 9-in-10-browse-on-purpose data for the c-store persona, and to the 43%-more-likely-to-try-if-sober-curious-framed data for the liquor persona.
- **Equity, not just top-spender rewards** — Gen Z loyalty research flags that programs rewarding only high spenders alienate this cohort; occasional/exploratory customers should still feel recognized. [Advantage Club]
- **Mobile-first, app-optional** — 43.5% prefer logging in via a mobile app specifically (higher than other generations), which is worth noting against Moolah's own no-app, phone-number-first architecture: for this specific audience, a lightweight web-based "my rewards" view accessible from the SMS link should feel app-like (fast, visual, mobile-native) even without requiring a download, since the underlying preference is for a good mobile experience, not necessarily an installed app.

### 2c. Content and channel strategy

- **TikTok and Instagram Reels are the discovery layer** — 77% of Gen Z use TikTok specifically to find products, and short-form video is where this cohort expects to encounter a store or brand before they ever walk in. Raw, unedited, "behind the counter" style content (already the recommended format in the RetailzPOS/Moolah social strategy) outperforms polished ads with this audience specifically.
- **Discord and BeReal are relationship-deepening, not acquisition, channels** — not a priority for initial capture, but worth monitoring as a place engaged, already-enrolled customers might organically discuss a store or product (e.g., a local Discord server for a college town's late-night c-store runs).
- **UGC is the highest-leverage content type for the c-store persona specifically** — "what's new at my corner store" browsing/haul-style content is already native behavior for this age group; the loyalty program should give them an explicit reason to film it (a bonus-points trigger for a photo/video share, for example) rather than trying to manufacture the content in-house.
- **For liquor content**, lean into the moderation-aligned framing directly — a "try the new low-ABV option, earn double points" campaign is both on-trend (43% purchase-intent lift from sober-curious framing) and inherently more defensible from a responsible-marketing standpoint than volume-based promotion.
- **For smoke/vape content**, keep messaging on store relationship, selection, and category-adjacent products (accessories, CBD/non-nicotine lines) rather than consumption prompts, given the 67%-want-to-quit data point — this is both the safer regulatory posture and, done well, a genuine point of differentiation ("the shop that treats you like a person, not a customer to upsell").

### 2d. Compliance guardrails (non-negotiable, ties directly to Blog #2)

- **21+ age-gating on every touchpoint** tied to alcohol or tobacco/vape content: ad targeting, landing pages, SMS opt-in flows, and any influencer partnership (25+ for the influencer themselves, per Meta's policy).
- **No paid retail/e-commerce alcohol-sale ads on TikTok** — the platform doesn't allow them. Organic, brand-level content (store culture, staff picks, general education) is the available lane there for the liquor vertical; paid acquisition for liquor should run on Meta/Google with correct 21+ targeting instead.
- **SHAFT-aware SMS copy** for any campaign touching the liquor or smoke/vape personas — route to a landing page rather than naming alcohol/tobacco products directly in the text body, consistent with the compliant-campaign example in Blog #2.
- **No youth-coded creative** in smoke/vape marketing specifically (cartoon-style branding, imagery that could read as appealing to a below-21 audience) — this is both a state-law flashpoint in a growing number of jurisdictions and a reputational risk independent of what's strictly legal.

### 2e. Where this plugs into the existing content strategy

This isn't a replacement for the 5-pillar content strategy already built (`moolahpoints-content-strategy-2026-07.md`) — it's an audience layer on top of it. Concretely:
- Pillar 3 ("The First Four Visits" / vertical proof) gets a Gen Z-specific angle: the c-store persona's high weekly-visit rate (3 in 4 visit weekly) makes the 4-visit-to-95%-return-rate stat land even faster for this audience than for the general population.
- Pillar 4 ("Text Beats App") should be nuanced for this audience specifically — the SMS-first case is still right (91-98% open rates don't change by generation), but the *destination* the text links to should feel mobile-app-quality, matching this cohort's specific preference for app-like experiences even without requiring an actual install.
- The Topic 5 social content set (c-store breakfast/beverage trend, already in the calendar) is a natural home for Persona C content — extend it with a "what's new, try it and earn double points" discovery angle.
