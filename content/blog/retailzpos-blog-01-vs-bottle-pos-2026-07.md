**Quick answer:** Both RetailzPOS and Bottle POS are liquor-specific point-of-sale systems built for independent bottle shops. The core difference is how each handles the two things that matter most in this category — inventory that scales cleanly from case to single bottle, and how loyalty/marketing is handled once a customer walks out the door. Bottle POS bundles a basic loyalty layer directly into the POS to avoid a separate purchase. RetailzPOS pairs with Moolah Points, a dedicated loyalty product with vertical-specific reward mechanics (like bonus points for trying a new wine), rather than a generic points counter bolted onto checkout.

## RetailzPOS vs. Bottle POS at a glance

**Choose RetailzPOS if** you want advanced loyalty marketing (SMS/email, referral rewards, tasting-event promotions), multi-store support, AI-powered inventory alerts, compliance-focused age verification, flexible payment processing, and room to grow beyond a single register.

**Choose Bottle POS if** you prefer an all-in-one bundled solution, only need basic loyalty tracking, want the simplicity of a single vendor relationship, and operate a straightforward single-location store where growth complexity isn't an immediate concern.

Neither answer is wrong — they're built for different stages of the same business. The rest of this comparison is about figuring out which stage you're actually in.

## Why this comparison matters

If you're running a liquor store, you already know most general retail POS systems weren't built with your category in mind. Case-break inventory, age verification that's actually enforced (not just a prompt a cashier can click past), and marketing that can legally reach customers about alcohol — these are liquor-specific problems, and both RetailzPOS and Bottle POS exist specifically to solve them. That's exactly why they get compared so often.

## Where they actually differ

**Inventory depth.** Both systems handle the liquor-specific problem of selling by the case and by the bottle — this isn't a differentiator, it's table stakes for a liquor-specific POS. Where RetailzPOS adds more: AI-driven reorder alerts that flag a fast-moving SKU before it runs out, rather than requiring a manual count.

**Loyalty: bundled vs. purpose-built.** This is the real fork in the road. Bottle POS's pitch, like most liquor-specific bundled systems in this category, is single-vendor simplicity — a loyalty feature that comes with the register instead of a separate purchase. (LMS-POS, a similar bundled competitor, [markets this explicitly as avoiding "expensive third-party marketing platforms"](https://lmspos.com/) — confirm Bottle POS's own current positioning directly, since that specific phrasing is LMS-POS's, not independently verified for Bottle POS.) That's a fair pitch if you want the absolute simplest single-vendor relationship and don't need much beyond basic points tracking. RetailzPOS takes a different bet: loyalty lives in a dedicated product (Moolah Points) built specifically around liquor-vertical mechanics — double points on a new varietal, referral perks, SMS campaigns written with [TCPA](https://www.fcc.gov/tags/telephone-consumer-protection-act-tcpa), [carrier SHAFT content restrictions](https://api.ctia.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/230523-CTIA-Messaging-Principles-and-Best-Practices-FINAL.pdf), and [DISCUS's responsible alcohol advertising guidelines](https://distilledspirits.org/code-of-responsible-practices/) in mind — rather than a generic points counter. If loyalty marketing is a real growth lever for your store (not just a nice-to-have), the dedicated-product approach usually has more room to grow with you.

**Compliance depth.** Age-restricted retail carries real regulatory exposure — for register-level age verification and for how you're legally allowed to text or email customers about alcohol. This is worth asking both vendors about directly and specifically, not assuming either one has it fully solved.

## Switching from Bottle POS to RetailzPOS: what to expect

Switching POS systems is a real decision with real friction, and it's worth addressing directly rather than glossing over:

- **Inventory import.** Your existing SKU list, case/bottle configurations, and stock counts should transfer as part of onboarding — confirm the specific import process and format requirements before your switch date, not after.
- **Customer and loyalty data migration.** If you're bringing existing customer records or loyalty balances over from Bottle POS, ask both what format that data can be exported in and how it maps into Moolah Points' reward structure.
- **Training.** Staff who've only used Bottle POS will need time on the new interface — ask what onboarding and training support is included, and plan a short overlap period rather than a hard cutover if possible.
- **Downtime.** Any POS migration carries some risk of disruption during cutover. Ask for a specific plan for your go-live day, including a fallback if something doesn't transfer cleanly.

None of this is a reason to avoid switching if RetailzPOS is genuinely the better fit — it's a reason to ask for a specific migration plan before you commit to a date, not after.

## Illustrative example: a growing liquor store

*The following is a composite, illustrative scenario built from common patterns in liquor retail — not a specific named customer case study.*

Picture a single-location liquor store that's been running on a bundled POS-and-loyalty system for a few years. The owner knows regulars by name, but the loyalty program itself is a flat points counter — buy X, get Y — with no way to do more than that. A new wine shipment comes in and there's no easy way to nudge past customers to try it. A quiet Tuesday afternoon has no mechanism to bring in a slow-season regular. The owner starts thinking about opening a second location and realizes the current system has no real answer for keeping inventory and loyalty data unified across two stores.

With a purpose-built loyalty layer like Moolah Points paired to RetailzPOS, the same store could run a same-day SMS campaign offering bonus points on the new wine shipment, set up an automated "we miss you" text triggered after a customer's usual visit window passes, and run a referral campaign around a tasting event — all without manually building each campaign from scratch, and with inventory and customer data unified if a second location opens.

This is the practical difference between "a loyalty feature" and "a loyalty program" — one tracks purchases, the other changes behavior.

## How the two approaches actually work (concept)

**Bottled loyalty (Bottle POS's model):** Customer buys → points added at checkout → customer redeems points on a future visit. A closed loop, entirely dependent on the customer coming back on their own.

**RetailzPOS + Moolah Points model:** Customer buys → points added at checkout → the purchase also triggers automated marketing logic (a new-product nudge, a win-back text after a missed visit window, a referral prompt) → customer receives a personalized, timed message → customer returns with a specific reason to. The loop actively pulls the customer back rather than waiting for them to remember.

## Buyer checklist

Print this or copy it into your notes before your next vendor call — for either system:

- [ ] Is age verification hard-blocked (transaction can't complete without a check) or soft-blocked (a skippable prompt)?
- [ ] How does case-to-single-unit inventory conversion work when a case is broken mid-shift?
- [ ] Does the platform account for alcohol-marketing content restrictions ([SHAFT](https://api.ctia.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/230523-CTIA-Messaging-Principles-and-Best-Practices-FINAL.pdf), [TCPA](https://www.fcc.gov/tags/telephone-consumer-protection-act-tcpa), [DISCUS](https://distilledspirits.org/code-of-responsible-practices/)) in SMS/email campaigns, or is that on you to figure out?
- [ ] What does data portability look like if you switch POS systems again later — can you export customer and inventory history cleanly?
- [ ] What reporting is available out of the box — sales by SKU, loyalty performance, compliance logs?
- [ ] What does the actual implementation timeline look like, including inventory and customer data migration?
- [ ] What payment processing options are available, and are you locked into one processor?
- [ ] What training and onboarding support is included, and for how long after go-live?

## FAQ

**Is Bottle POS or RetailzPOS better for a single-location liquor store?**
Both are built for independent liquor retail and handle case-break inventory. The deciding factor is usually how much you want loyalty/marketing to do — Bottle POS bundles a basic version in; RetailzPOS pairs with a dedicated loyalty product (Moolah Points) built for the vertical.

**Do either of these systems handle age verification automatically?**
Ask both vendors directly and specifically — this varies and matters more than almost any other feature in liquor retail, since a missed check carries real fine and license risk.

**Can I run SMS marketing campaigns through either platform?**
RetailzPOS's loyalty layer (Moolah Points) is built with alcohol-marketing content restrictions ([SHAFT](https://api.ctia.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/230523-CTIA-Messaging-Principles-and-Best-Practices-FINAL.pdf), [TCPA](https://www.fcc.gov/tags/telephone-consumer-protection-act-tcpa), and [DISCUS's alcohol advertising code](https://distilledspirits.org/code-of-responsible-practices/)) in mind. Confirm directly with Bottle POS what their SMS/email capabilities include.

**What's involved in switching from Bottle POS to RetailzPOS?**
Inventory import, customer/loyalty data migration, staff training, and a planned go-live cutover. Ask for a specific migration plan — including how existing data transfers and what the training timeline looks like — before committing to a switch date.

**Is this comparison biased since RetailzPOS wrote it?**
Fair question — we're transparent that this comes from the RetailzPOS Product Team, and it's written to help you ask sharper questions of both vendors, not to hide Bottle POS's legitimate strengths. Verify anything that matters to your decision directly with both companies.
