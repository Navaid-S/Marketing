**Quick answer:** Square for Retail is a low-friction, well-designed POS that's genuinely good for a small store with straightforward needs — easy setup, transparent pricing, and Square Loyalty available as a card-linked add-on (roughly $45-49/month per location). Where it shows real limits for liquor, vape, and convenience retailers specifically: case-break inventory, category-specific loyalty mechanics, and purpose-built age-verification enforcement aren't part of what Square was designed around.

## Square for Retail vs. RetailzPOS at a glance

**Choose Square if** you want fast setup, transparent published pricing, and you're running general merchandise with minimal age-restricted product complexity.

**Choose RetailzPOS if** case-break inventory, category-specific loyalty mechanics, and register-level age-verification enforcement are real, active requirements for your store.

## What Square for Retail does well

Square earns its popularity honestly. Setup is fast, pricing is transparent and published (unusual in this category — most competitors make you request a quote), and the interface is genuinely easy for staff to learn. If you're running general merchandise with a moderate SKU count and don't have complex age-restricted-product needs, Square is a legitimately solid, low-risk choice.

**Square Loyalty**, the add-on program, is card-linked (no separate app or physical card needed) and priced around $45-49/month per location — straightforward and reasonably affordable. It performs best in food & beverage and general retail use cases, per Square's own reported category breakdown.

## Where it runs into limits for specialty retail

**Inventory depth for case-break categories.** Liquor retail specifically requires clean case-to-bottle conversion — selling a case, breaking it into singles, and having inventory counts stay accurate through that process. Square's inventory tools are built for general retail, not this specific workflow, and it shows once your SKU count and case/bottle mix grows.

**Loyalty mechanics are generic.** Square Loyalty is a solid, affordable add-on, but it's a standard points/visit system — there's no equivalent to category-specific reward triggers (a bonus for a new wine, a vape-and-e-liquid bundle, a coffee-and-breakfast pairing) that actually shape purchase behavior rather than just tracking it.

**Age verification isn't purpose-built.** Square doesn't ship with liquor/tobacco/vape-specific hard-block age-gating the way a system designed around regulated retail does. If your compliance posture depends on staff remembering to check ID rather than the register enforcing it, that's worth addressing directly — see our [POS compliance guide] for why that distinction matters more than almost any other setting in this category.

## When Square is genuinely the right call

If you're a small, single-location store with simple, low-complexity inventory and minimal age-restricted product exposure, Square's ease of use and transparent pricing are real advantages — don't switch away from something that's working just because a specialty competitor exists. The calculation changes once case-break inventory, multi-SKU age-restricted product lines, or real compliance exposure enter the picture.

## Switching from Square to RetailzPOS: what to expect

## FAQ

**Is Square for Retail good for a liquor store?**
It can work for a simple, low-SKU operation, but case-break inventory and age-verification enforcement aren't purpose-built the way they are in a liquor-specific POS — worth testing directly against your actual product mix before committing.

**How much does Square Loyalty cost?**
Roughly $45-49/month per location, card-linked, no separate app required. Confirm current pricing directly with Square.

**What's the biggest reason a convenience or liquor store owner outgrows Square?**
Most commonly: inventory complexity (case-break, multi-SKU age-restricted lines) and a need for compliance enforcement or loyalty mechanics more specific than Square's general-purpose tools provide.

**What's involved in switching from Square?**
Inventory export and remapping, loyalty data migration, checking for app-marketplace dependencies, and staff training on new compliance/loyalty features. Ask for a specific migration plan before committing to a cutover date.

**Is this review biased since RetailzPOS wrote it?**
Fair question — we're transparent about that, and we've tried to give Square credit for what it does well (ease of use, transparent pricing) rather than dismissing it outright. Verify anything that matters to your decision directly with Square.
