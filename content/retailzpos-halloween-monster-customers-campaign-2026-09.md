# The concept

Classic Halloween monsters walk into the store as customers, each ordering something that thematically matches their persona — a vampire asking for a Bloody Mary kit, a werewolf asking for moonshine. The joke mechanically makes the point: six completely different, oddly specific orders in a row is exactly the scenario a well-stocked, always-ready shelf is built for. Tagline: **"This spooky season, be ready for any kind of customer."**

---

# 1. The monster lineup

## The Vampire → Bloody Mary kit
Glides up to the counter, cape dramatic: *"I'll need everything for a Bloody Mary. Emphasis on the Mary."*
Cashier already has the vodka, tomato juice, and garnish kit staged. The obvious pun, but obvious because it's perfect — the anchor monster of the set.

## The Werewolf → Moonshine
Scruffy, glances at the sky: *"Full moon's out. Gonna need something that hits like moonshine — actually, just moonshine."*
Clean, one-beat pun (full moon → moonshine), and a real, specific specialty SKU that's easy to be caught short on.

## The Witch → Absinthe
Mysterious, cauldron energy: *"Something green. Something strong. Something that turns you a little... funny."*
The smartest pairing in the set — absinthe's real folklore nickname is "The Green Fairy," a genuine historical tie to witch/potion mythology, not just a color joke. Good "did you know" caption material.

## The Mummy → Spiced rum
Shuffling, wrapped in bandages: *"5,000 years wrapped up, and I still know good spice when I smell it. Spiced rum, please."*
Lesser-obvious but historically grounded — real mummification used aromatic spices (cinnamon, myrrh, frankincense).

## The Zombie → a "Zombie" cocktail
Groaning, shuffling in: *"Braaains— no, wait. Just a Zombie. The drink. Obviously."*
Self-aware gag — the "Zombie" is a real, famous tiki cocktail (a heavy rum blend), so the zombie ordering the drink literally named after it is the easiest, most satisfying punchline of the set.

## Frankenstein's Monster → Red Bull vodka
Flat affect, sudden spark: *"Need something with a jolt. Whatever's got the most voltage."*
Clean electricity pun, and a nice tonal change of pace from the classic-cocktail energy of the other five — closes the lineup on something a little more modern.

# 2. Format options

## Flagship video (30–40 seconds)
One continuous bit at the same register — a different costumed "customer" walks up every 5-6 seconds, delivers their line, the cashier rings it up without missing a beat each time. The cashier's deadpan competence is the joke. Cheap to shoot: one set, one cashier, six quick costume changes. Running six vignettes at ~5-6 seconds each puts the full cut around 35-40 seconds — comfortably inside the flagship-length target.

**Closing line:** *"You never know who's walking in this spooky season. RetailzPOS makes sure you're never caught without it."* End card: logo + "Book a demo."

**Caption:** "Every customer's a little different this time of year. Good thing the shelf doesn't know the difference."

**Hashtags:** #RetailzPOS #HalloweenMarketing #LiquorStoreLife #POSsoftware

**Also runs on:** Instagram Reels (primary), TikTok (mirror), Facebook (mirror, native upload).

## Carousel
One monster per slide — order, one-liner, and a small "did you know" note where the lore is real (witch/absinthe, mummy/spices). Final slide ties it to the actual feature: *"Six different customers, six completely different orders — this is exactly what AI reorder alerts and case-break inventory are built for."*

## Six-day drip series
One monster a day through the week of Halloween instead of one big post — turns a single idea into six days of content and builds a "which one's next" curiosity loop. Ending on Frankenstein the day before or the day of Halloween works well as a change-of-pace closer after five classic-cocktail beats in a row.

## Poll
"Which monster's order matches YOUR go-to drink?" — playful, zero compliance risk, doubles as real customer-preference signal.

## Quote card
The tagline itself, standalone: *"This spooky season, be ready for any kind of customer."* Gold text on Ink Charcoal, works independent of the video.

# 3. Direction notes

- Costumes can be cheap/minimal — a cape, a bandage wrap, green face paint — the writing and the cashier's deadpan reaction are doing the comedic work, not production value.
- Keep every order phrased as a product/ingredient request, never as a joke about intoxication or overconsumption — each monster is buying ingredients for a drink they'd make at home, not drinking on camera. This keeps the whole bit squarely in the "clever shopping trip" territory rather than anywhere near a compliance concern.
- If shot as the continuous-take video, rehearse the cashier's ring-up timing so each transaction actually looks fluid — the joke depends on the cashier never being thrown off, which visually sells "always stocked and ready" better than any line of dialogue could.
