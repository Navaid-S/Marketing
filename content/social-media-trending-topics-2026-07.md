## 1. Trending Topics Summary

Seven angles rose to the top — a mix of **compliance/pain-point hooks**, **buying-intent pain points**, and **content formats** currently winning engagement. All are chosen because they intersect directly with RetailzPOS's ICP: independent liquor stores, convenience stores, smoke shops, and small multi-location chains.

|---|-------|--------------------------------------|

Built-in, automated age verification is a high-stakes, high-relevance value prop, not a generic feature pitch. |
| 2 | **The Great Inventory Reckoning** — 85% of retailers still run inventory on spreadsheets, and convenience stores alone lose 1-4% of revenue to shrinkage (with 20% of that traced to bad record-keeping, not theft) on top of $1.7T in annual global stock-distortion losses ([inFlow](https://www.inflowinventory.com/blog/state-of-inventory-management-2026/), [InVue](https://invue.com/resource-center/blog/6-retail-shrinkage-statistics), [VNDLY](https://www.vndly.io/blog/inventory-shrinkage-statistics-2026)) | This is RetailzPOS's core value prop stated in the market's own words — a ready-made pain-point hook with a stat that stops the scroll, and shrinkage is a category liquor stores, c-stores, and smoke shops all fight constantly. |
| 3 | **Tap-to-Pay & Rush-Hour Throughput** — over 65% of in-person card transactions are now contactless, and in convenience, liquor, and smoke shop retail — where the average trip is under two minutes — checkout speed during rush hours (morning coffee-and-cigarette run, Friday night beer run) directly drives revenue ([Host Merchant Services](https://hostmerchantservices.com/articles/retail-payment-trends-2/)) | Payments friction costs sales fastest in these verticals, where most trips are quick, single-purpose stops. A checkout that's fast AND fully age-compliant in one motion is a strong, concrete differentiator. |
| 4 | **AI for the Rest of Us** — retail AI in 2026 has shifted from hype to "practical AI agents" and vertical-specific tools built for a specific industry outperform generic AI, while owners still want "real human voices" in their content ([Capgemini](https://www.capgemini.com/insights/research-library/from-hype-to-how-retail-ai-trends-2026/), [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/terdawn-deboe/2026/01/02/15-ai-predictions-for-small-businesses-in-2026/)) | RetailzPOS can own "AI that actually helps a shop owner" instead of vague AI hype — differentiates from generic retail-AI noise. |
