One system for every lead we generate.

A recommendation to adopt HubSpot as the shared system of record connecting marketing campaigns to sales conversion — across RetailzPOS, Moolah Points, and PtechPOS.

The problem

We can't currently see the line between ad spend and revenue.

  • Three products, three separate motions. Marketing runs campaigns for RetailzPOS, Moolah Points, and PtechPOS, and today there's no single record of which campaign actually produced a paying customer — only which one produced a click.
  • Leads don't become anything actionable on their own. A conversion on a tagged landing page doesn't automatically turn into something sales can act on — it sits until someone manually reviews and forwards it, and the campaign data that explains where it came from often doesn't travel with it.
  • Sales has no consistent way to triage. Reps have no reliable signal for which inbound leads are worth calling first, so real selling time goes into manual sorting instead.
  • We can't answer the one question that matters most. "What did it cost us to acquire our last ten customers, and which campaign brought them in" currently requires stitching together ad-platform exports by hand.

This is a systems problem, not a headcount or a talent problem — and it has a specific, well-understood fix.

The ask

Adopt one CRM, shared by marketing and sales, across all three products.

We're recommending HubSpot — starting on the Starter tier — as the single system that captures a lead the moment they convert from a tagged campaign, automatically flags it as marketing-qualified once it meets our criteria, hands it to a sales rep in real time with full campaign context attached, and reports the resulting numbers back to both teams in one dashboard.

The mechanism

From a tagged click to a rep's queue, automatically.

No manual forwarding, no lost campaign data, no lag between "this person is interested" and "someone is calling them."

01
Campaign
A UTM-tagged Google or Meta ad drives a click to a product landing page.
02
Capture
The form writes source, medium, and campaign data onto the contact record automatically.
03
Qualify
A workflow checks the lead against our MQL criteria and flips the lifecycle stage the moment it's met.
04
Hand off
The lead is auto-assigned to a rep and a real-time notification goes out with the full campaign trail attached.
05
Convert
The rep works the deal through the same pipeline, and every stage is visible to marketing as it happens.
What changes

Marketing, sales, and leadership working off the same record.

Marketing
  • UTM capture and campaign attribution happen automatically, not by hand
  • MQL becomes a real, shared definition — not a spreadsheet argument with sales
  • Visibility into what happens to a lead after it's handed off, not just at the point of capture
Sales
  • Leads arrive already qualified, not raw and unsorted
  • Full context — source campaign, product line, on-site behavior — before the first call
  • One queue, real-time notification, no shared inbox to monitor
Leadership
  • One dashboard covering cost per lead, cost per MQL, and conversion rate
  • Answers broken out by product line — RetailzPOS, Moolah Points, PtechPOS
  • No more waiting on a manual reporting cycle to know what's working
What we can finally measure

Four numbers we currently can't produce on demand.

Cost per Lead
By campaign, channel, and product line — not a blended, after-the-fact estimate.
Cost per MQL
The number that tells us whether a campaign is producing real interest, not just clicks.
MQL → SQL Conversion Rate
Where the marketing-to-sales handoff is actually working, and where it isn't.
Cost per Customer
The number that actually justifies — or kills — a campaign's budget.

We don't have a baseline for any of these today. Phase 1 (below) exists specifically to establish one, so future budget conversations run on real numbers instead of estimates.
