# Moolah Points — Brand Guide (Summary)
**Full interactive guide:** `moolahpoints-brand-guide-2026-07.html` (published as an Artifact — color system, typography specimens, logo mark, mood board, and post templates, all live-rendered with real embedded fonts).

Built from the real brand elements visible in Moolah's existing Instagram creative (cream/parchment ground, mustard-gold, charcoal, the triple-mark logo, and the gold-teal "glowing token" reward-moment photography), extended with a color-theory-grounded complement and two supporting accents, researched against 2026 fintech/loyalty color trends.

Concept: the whole guide is framed as a **ledger/receipt** — itemized sections (§01–§07), a torn-edge hero, monospace "line item" tags — since Moolah Points is literally a company about points, receipts, and currency. This is a deliberate structural choice, not decoration.

## Color system

| Token | Hex | Role | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ledger Cream | `#F6EEDC` | Primary ground (~60% of any layout) | The receipt paper — warm, not sterile |
| Ink Charcoal | `#221F1A` | Primary text (~27%) | Warm-biased near-black, softer than pure #000 on cream |
| Moolah Gold | `#D6A429` | Primary accent (~8%) | The coin — currency, achievement, reward. Accent only, never body text |
| Token Teal | `#14625F` | Complementary accent (~3%) | Gold sits near 42° on the hue wheel; true complement is ~222° (blue), but teal (~178°) is a softer near-complement that's *already* the color of Moolah's own glowing-token photography, and matches WGSN's 2026 fintech card forecast (Amber Haze + Transformative Teal) |
| Cellar Burgundy | `#7A2436` | Vertical accent, liquor-specific (~2%) | Wine-dark; single-accent alternate for liquor-vertical posts — never paired with Teal in the same asset |
| Warm Slate | `#8C8272` | Secondary neutral | Rules, borders, captions — a warm-biased gray, chosen rather than defaulted to |

**Usage ratio:** ~60% cream / 27% charcoal / 8% gold / 5% teal+burgundy combined. If gold and teal are both fighting for attention in one layout, drop one — see the wheel diagram in the full guide for why they're a *near*-complementary pair, not a true complementary one, and why that matters for restraint.

Dark mode is a deliberate "night register" reinterpretation (deep ink ground, brightened gold/teal for contrast), not a naive color inversion — toggle it in the artifact via the theme button top-right.

## Typography

| Role | Typeface | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Display | **Anton** | Headlines, section titles, hero statements — all caps only |
| Body | **Nunito** (400–900) | Subheadlines, captions, UI copy, long-form text |
| Utility | **Space Mono** (400, 700) | Hex codes, data callouts, item tags, small print — the "receipt printer" voice |

All three are real, licensable Google Fonts (free for commercial use), embedded live in the artifact so the specimens render exactly as intended rather than falling back to system fonts.

## Logo & mark

Three overlapping "D"-shaped forms — reads as stacked coins in profile, or as quotation marks (the brand talking back to its merchants). Three colorways specified (gold-on-cream default, charcoal-on-light, cream-on-ink for dark posts), plus clearspace and minimum-size rules — all detailed in the full guide.

## Mood board & post templates

The full guide includes a 6-tile mood board (token glow, ledger grain, teal depth, cellar-to-coin gradient, cellar wood, hologram) built from the same palette, plus two fully-styled post-template mockups (liquor and convenience examples) showing the recurring layout system with a labeled photo-panel placeholder and a compact AI image-generation prompt for completing each.
