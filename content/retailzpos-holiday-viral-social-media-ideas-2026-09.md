# How to use this list

Every idea below leans on an actual viral *mechanic* — a duet chain, a countdown, a UGC contest, a trend-format hijack — rather than just "post about the holiday." Each one notes the format, the hook, and why it has real spread potential. See the companion "Ghost of POS Past" campaign document for a fully worked-out Halloween concept built the same way.

---

# Halloween

**1. "Costume vs. ID" reveal.** Split-screen: an elaborate costume next to the same person's driver's license photo, cashier double-take, then a calm cut to the hard-block verification screen handling it regardless of the wig or contacts. *"Costume's incredible. Verification doesn't care."* Rides the reveal-cut format that's everywhere right now.

**2. "Rating tonight's costumes" — a recurring bit, not a one-off.** Staff judges costumes 1-10 like a panel show, filmed live at the register through the last week of October. "Rating things" is one of the most reliably viral evergreen formats on the platform, and it turns one idea into 5-6 days of content instead of one post.

**3. The "documentary parody" trend, Halloween edition.** Dead-serious, cinematic-music mock interview: *"The scariest thing isn't the costumes. It's almost running out of the good stuff at 9pm."* Cut to the reorder alert catching it in time. Zero production cost, rides a trend that's live right now.

**4. Store-to-store costume-judging duet chain.** Post your own costume-judging clip and challenge the next store to duet/stitch it. The one with real network-effect potential — every participating store multiplies the reach for free, and it's genuinely easy for any independent retailer to join.

**5. Candy-and-drink pairing carousel.** Candy corn, chocolate, caramel apples each paired with something on the shelf. Pure Entertain, shareable, zero compliance surface.

# Thanksgiving / Black Friday / Small Business Saturday

**6. "Wednesday before Thanksgiving" chaos-then-calm video.** The "win is a win" trend format: bad news, the whole town showed up at once; win, the reorder dashboard already saw it coming three days earlier.

**7. Small Business Saturday shoutout chain.** Ask customers to tag the next local independent business they love. Classic tag-3 mechanic, and it fits the actual premise of the day almost too perfectly.

**8. "Things this store will never do on Black Friday."** Deadpan list, "kinda chic to ___" trend energy: *"kinda chic to not trample anyone for a discount."* Distinguishes from big-box culture with humor instead of a lecture.

**9. Countdown: "What sells out first on Thanksgiving Eve."** Top-5 countdown format, builds suspense, #1 ties back to a real reorder-alert save.

**10. UGC prompt: "rate my Friendsgiving pairing."** Customers post their own dessert-wine pairing photos tagging the store; simple contest mechanic, real user-generated reach.

# Christmas / Hanukkah / gifting

**11. "Gift or regift?" guessing game.** Hold up a bottle, guess whether it reads as a real gift or an office white-elephant regift. Playful, about gifting culture, not consumption.

**12. "8 nights, 8 bottles" — a Hanukkah carousel series.** One SKU or pairing per night. Genuinely rare in this category (most liquor content skews Christmas-only), and it's real audience expansion, not just a nice gesture.

**13. ASMR gift-wrapping video.** Satisfying, sensory wrapping content for a bottle gift set. The wrapping/packing ASMR genre performs consistently well, and it's a direct, low-lift visual version of the gift-bundling idea already in the holiday blog plan.

**14. "Meet the teammate who remembers everyone's holiday order" — holiday cut of the coworker-introduction trend.** Personifies Moolah Points' win-back automation in a festive frame.

**15. Staff white-elephant gift exchange, filmed.** Pure culture content. Office gift-exchange videos are an evergreen viral genre, and it humanizes the brand heading into the most transactional week of the year.

# New Year's Eve / Day

**16. Real-time sparkling wine countdown.** A Stories series checking shelf stock every few hours through NYE day, building appointment-viewing suspense out of actual inventory data.

**17. "New Year, New Me" flipped to the business.** Before/after meme format: *"New Year, Same Me: still hand-counting inventory at midnight. New Year, New Store: hasn't touched a clipboard since [feature]."*

**18. "Resolutions we're keeping" list.** Sincere-but-funny: *"Still won't tell you the price over the phone. Still will always answer honestly."* Reinforces the pricing-policy voice without ever sounding defensive about it.

**19. "The last hour before midnight" chaos video.** High energy, ends on a calm, undramatic line that the hard-block doesn't care what time it is — a compliance mention that never feels like a compliance mention.

**20. New Year's Day contrast post.** The loudest night of the year vs. the quietest morning after. Simple pacing/contrast content that closes the seasonal arc.
