# The concept

A mockumentary confessional — the same dead-serious "documentary interview" format currently trending on social — built around two personified systems giving reality-show-style exit interviews about how they handled the holidays. No nightmare, no waking up. The contrast between a shell-shocked old POS and a completely serene RetailzPOS does the same persuasive job as a nightmare-and-relief structure, through personality and comedic timing instead of a horror-adjacent story beat.

**Why this is structurally different, not just a re-skin of the Holiday Rush Nightmare concept:** there's no dream, no jump-scare, no wake-up beat. The entire device is personality and contrast through a format the audience already recognizes as funny *right now*, rather than borrowing tension from a decades-old ad trope (nightmare-then-relief). It's also easier to spin into a series without repeating itself — see Section 3.

# 1. Flagship spot (45 seconds)

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:04 | Hard interview lighting, single chair, dramatic pause music. Text on screen: *"Exit Interview: Holiday Season 2026."* |
| 0:05–0:20 | **The old POS, "interviewed" in first person** by the store owner playing the role — shell-shocked, staring into the middle distance like a just-eliminated reality show contestant: *"I just... I don't know what happened out there. Black Friday hit and I just froze. Every register, on me. I wasn't ready."* Single dramatic pause. Maybe a single tear, played completely straight. |
| 0:21–0:24 | Quick cutaway B-roll of "what it's confessing to" — the actual chaos, edited like a reality-show flashback. This is the exact structural device real docuseries use: talking head, then cutaway proof. |
| 0:25–0:40 | **Hard cut, same lighting, completely different energy — RetailzPOS's own "exit interview."** Same actor or a second one, totally serene, maybe sipping coffee: *"The holidays? Honestly? Pretty chill. Saw the rush coming three days out. Restocked. Handled the line. Went home on time."* Small, satisfied shrug. |
| 0:41–0:45 | Text card: *"This holiday season, don't run on a system that needs a support group."* End card: logo + *"Switch to RetailzPOS. Book a demo."* |

**Caption:** "We interviewed both POS systems after the holidays. Only one of them needed a moment."

**Hashtags:** #RetailzPOS #HolidayRush #POSsoftware #ExitInterview

**Also runs on:** Instagram Reels/TikTok (primary — this format is built for short-form), Facebook (mirror).

**Direction:** One actor can play both roles for a funnier bit — same person, two completely different energies, maybe a visual tell like a different jacket or lighting temperature. No props needed beyond a chair and a fake interview backdrop; the entire budget is acting choices and edit rhythm.

---

# 2. Casting and tone notes

- Play both "interviews" completely straight — the comedy comes from treating a POS system's holiday season with the emotional weight of an actual reality-show elimination, not from mugging at the camera.
- The old POS's confessional should feel genuinely a little sad, not just chaotic — that's what sells the parody of the format.
- RetailzPOS's confessional should never feel smug or superior in delivery — calm and slightly amused is funnier and more on-brand than gloating, and keeps it from reading as a dig at real customers who may still be on an older system.

---

# 3. Series potential — "exit interviews" with other characters

The same format, format-locked at 15 seconds each, works as a recurring bit without repeating the joke:

| Character | The confession |
|---|---|
| The fax machine | Overwhelmed by a season of invoices, spiraling about paper jams |
| The invoice binder | Falling apart at the seams, numbers that never added up |
| The register tape | Ran out three times during the biggest weekend of the year |
| The old inventory spreadsheet | Hasn't been updated since Halloween, in denial about it |

Each ends on the same RetailzPOS confessional cutaway, reused across the series — meaning one calm "RetailzPOS" interview segment can be filmed once and intercut with a new "old system" confession each time, keeping production cost low across a multi-week run.

---

# 4. A third flavor, not yet fully developed: bait-and-switch

Open on what looks like total holiday-season disaster footage — frantic customers, an overwhelmed owner, disaster-movie-trailer music — and just as it peaks, reveal it's actually a completely normal, calm Tuesday, because RetailzPOS already handled everything the footage implied was chaos. This subverts the nightmare expectation entirely rather than fulfilling it, a third distinct mechanic alongside the Holiday Rush Nightmare (fulfills the nightmare, then resolves it) and this Exit Interview concept (skips the nightmare, plays it for character comedy instead). Worth developing as its own document if useful.
